# Book 1 to Book 3 Mapping

*Editorial note. This is a review document, not a finished reference. It exists to answer one question directly: for every theoretical construct Book 1 defines, where does Book 3 actually implement it, and where doesn't it? Book 1 citations refer to Book 1's own, unchanged chapter numbers throughout (Book 1 has not been renumbered — only its Work Item entity was renamed to Deliverable, in place). Book 3 citations refer to Book 3 (Refined)'s current chapter numbers, current as of the 2026-08-03 renumbering that inserted a new Chapter 1 (Objective) and a new Chapter 11 (Service). Read the "Key Findings" section first — it's the short version. Everything after it is the evidence for those findings, organized the way Book 1 itself is organized (SEU Structure → SEU Loops → Workbench), so you can trace any specific claim back to its source.*

---

## Key Findings

**1. "Work Item" meant opposite things in the two books — RESOLVED 2026-08-03: Book 1's Work Item renamed to Deliverable.**

- Book 1's **Activity** (Ch. 13) was, and remains, "the smallest unit of organised work... transient... participants execute them, but the organisation defines them." Book 1's own **Work Item** (Ch. 22) was explicitly *not* that — it was "a **persistent** organisational entity," "the execution aggregate that binds [all eleven entities] together for a single piece of organisational intent."
- Book 3 uses the same term, "Work Item," for the *other* concept: Book 3's Work Item (Ch. 32) is "a transient, Participant-specific execution artefact... explicitly not persistent," while Book 3's **Deliverable** (Ch. 2, 9, 15) is the persistent, primary aggregate that everything else attaches to.
- The correspondence was: **Book 1 Activity ↔ Book 3 Work Item**, and **Book 1 Work Item ↔ Book 3 Deliverable** — same two concepts, same relationship between them, names swapped, which would have produced exactly backwards conclusions for anyone reading both books together.
- **Resolution applied:** at Sudha's direct instruction, Book 1's Work Item has been renamed **Deliverable** throughout — Chapter 22 (now "The Deliverable") and every cross-reference in Chapters 21, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, the Part 4 Introduction and the Foreword. Book 1's Activity (Ch. 13) is unchanged and already matched Book 3's Work Item. **The two books now use identical terminology for both concepts** — Activity/Work Item for the transient unit, Deliverable for the persistent aggregate. See `01_Book 1 (Refined)/_Editorial Log.md` (2026-08-03 entry) for the full change list, and Chapter 14 §14.6 for the one follow-on content fix the rename required (Artefact vs. Deliverable, previously contrasted against the old informal sense of "deliverable").

**2. Service (Book 1's third foundational entity) had no Book 3 counterpart — RESOLVED 2026-08-03: new Chapter 11 (Service) added.**

Book 1 gives Service its own narrative chapter (Ch. 7, "The Value Exchange Model") and formal chapter (Ch. 10), and the Capability Reasoning Network chapter (Ch. 20) treats Service as one of the four things capabilities exchange (alongside Evidence, Knowledge, Decision) — "Requirements Engineering provides an Approved Requirements service that Architecture consumes." Book 3 had no Service Model chapter at all.

**Resolution applied:** Service is now Book 3's Chapter 11 — the Capability Pack's declared, contracted output, sharpening the Dependency Engine's (Ch. 9 §8) "Capability Dependency" edges from vague capability-availability into a concrete, named contract, and carrying its own declared Service Level as a measurable characteristic that Engineering Telemetry (Ch. 35 §9) derives from, alongside its other existing sources — deliberately *not* a fifth coordination channel that would crowd out Evidence, Knowledge and Decision, consistent with Book 1's own CRN chapter keeping Service as one of four coequal channels, not a governing hub. Chapter 9 and Chapter 35 were both updated with reciprocal cross-references.

**3. Objective is assumed everywhere in Book 3, defined nowhere — RESOLVED 2026-08-03: new Chapter 1 (Objective) added.**

Book 1 makes Objective the root of everything — every other entity "must ultimately trace back to one or more" Objectives (Ch. 4 §4.2), and it gets its own narrative (Ch. 5) and formal (Ch. 8) chapters. Book 3's SEU chapter (now Ch. 2) says an SEU is "commissioned to achieve one or more software engineering objectives," and Templates/Profiles/Deliverables all implicitly reference objectives — but there was no Objective Model chapter, and Objective wasn't in the Canonical Information Model.

**Resolution applied:** Objective is now Book 3's Chapter 1 — ahead of SEU, EBM and Template Model, since an Objective's declared or derived required Capabilities are what Template Model (Ch. 6) validates or selects against, and what the Composition Engine (Ch. 4) ultimately composes Packs to fulfil. Objective is the actual driver of commissioning decisions, not just a preamble to them. Chapter 2 (SEU) was updated with a reciprocal cross-reference.

**4. Governance's "Standard" half was missing — RESOLVED 2026-08-03: absorbed into Policy as a Constraint Type.**

Book 1 splits Governance into **Policy** (mandatory constraint) and **Standard** (preferred convention) — both named explicitly (Ch. 4 §4.2, Ch. 18). Book 3 had a full Policy Model (Ch. 24) but nothing corresponding to "preferred, not mandatory" convention as its own governed, versioned object.

**Resolution applied:** rather than a second entity, Policy (Ch. 24) now declares a **Constraint Type** — "Policy" (mandatory, blocks the governed transition on violation) or "Standard" (preferred, deviation does not block). The two share identical structure — Category, Applicability, Conditions, Required Evidence, Exception Rules, Version, Originating Pack — so a second entity would have duplicated the whole chapter for one attribute's worth of difference, the same reasoning that led Quality Gate and Obligation to unify their own traditionally-separate concepts. Constraint Type is deliberately independent of the existing Severity field: one determines whether a violation blocks anything, the other how much it matters. Governance (Ch. 21 §10), Quality Gate (Ch. 26 §9) and Engineering Telemetry (Ch. 35 §7, a new "Standard adherence rate" metric alongside "Policy compliance rate") were each updated with the corresponding reference.

**5. Continuous Organisational Learning was measured, not driven — RESOLVED 2026-08-03: Telemetry now raises Organisational Learning Obligations.**

Book 1's Continuous Organisational Learning chapter (now Ch. 21, relocated to Part 3 as SEU Loops' third loop on 2026-08-03) treats organisational learning as an active capability — the organisation gets measurably better over time because Knowledge accumulation feeds back into Capability improvement. Book 3's Engineering Telemetry (Ch. 35) measured the right things ("Knowledge growth," "Decision reuse") but nothing took those measurements and actually improved a Capability definition. This was the gap most directly relevant to whether Book 3 is "implementable" in the sense of doing what Book 1 promises, versus just recording data that a human would have to act on manually.

**Resolution applied:** no new persistent object was needed. Obligation (Ch. 23) already unifies risk, audit finding, tech debt and compliance gap into one governed-commitment concept with a resolution lifecycle — Organisational Learning is now one more category in that same list. Engineering Telemetry (Ch. 35 §11) raises an Organisational Learning Obligation when it detects a sustained pattern (a repeatedly-independently-reached Decision, a Service chronically missing its Service Level, a repeatedly-waived Policy). Resolving that Obligation produces a revised Capability, Service or Policy Pack version, which the Composition Engine recomposes into a new Effective Engineering Configuration (Ch. 10 §13, Capability Evolution) — closing the loop from measurement to actual improved future behaviour using machinery the platform already had (Obligation, Pack versioning, Composition Engine), rather than inventing a fourth new object. Captured as **ADR – Telemetry-Driven Organisational Learning** in the Architecture Catalogue.

**6. Knowledge Acquisition (the process) isn't modelled — only Knowledge (the object) is.**

Book 1 spends three chapters (26, 28, 29) on how observation becomes accepted Knowledge — discovery, validation, refinement, legacy recovery, native creation. Book 3's Knowledge Model (Ch. 16) specifies the object and its lifecycle once accepted, not the acquisition pipeline that gets it there. Likely fine to leave as an implementation detail Packs would define, but worth confirming that's the intended reading rather than an oversight. Not yet acted on.

**7. ORC and CRN are implemented but never named.**

This one isn't a gap — it's an opportunity. Book 1's Organisational Reasoning Cycle (Activities → Artefacts → Evidence → Knowledge → Decisions → Activities) is, under the Finding-1 renaming, exactly what Book 3's Execution Engine (Ch. 31) + Dependency Engine (Ch. 9) + Evidence/Knowledge/Decision Models actually do at runtime, and Book 3's own "Trust Pipeline" ADR is a tighter version of the cycle's middle stages. The Capability Reasoning Network (many capabilities coordinating through Service/Evidence/Knowledge/Decision, never directly) matches Book 3's Engineering Collaboration Model (Ch. 14) almost exactly, including the "no direct participant-to-participant messaging" rule. None of this is contradicted anywhere — it's just never cross-referenced, so a reader of both books has to notice the match themselves. Worth a one-line pointer in the relevant Book 3 chapters once Findings 2-3 are settled. Not yet acted on.
