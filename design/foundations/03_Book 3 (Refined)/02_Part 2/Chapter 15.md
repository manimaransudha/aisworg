Originally, we thought the runtime revolved around **Work Items**. Then we shifted to **Dependencies**. I now think both are supporting concepts.

The actual runtime revolves around **state transitions of Deliverables**.

The execution loop now looks like this:

```
Deliverable State

↓

Dependency Evaluation

↓

Capability Requirement

↓

Capability Fulfilment

↓

Participant Execution

↓

Evidence

↓

Governance

↓

Deliverable State Transition
```

This is fundamentally different from traditional workflow engines, which execute predefined sequences of tasks. In this architecture, **the state of the Deliverable drives execution**, and everything else exists to enable or validate that state transition.

I think this has an important consequence for the remaining chapters.

**Work Items should no longer be modelled as persistent business objects.**

Instead, they should be treated as **ephemeral execution plans** generated on demand to move a Deliverable from one state to the next. They are analogous to an execution plan generated by a database query optimiser: useful while executing, but not the primary object of the system.

If we adopt that principle, the remainder of the platform becomes even cleaner:

- Deliverables are persistent.
- Knowledge is persistent.
- Decisions are persistent.
- Evidence is persistent.
- Obligations are persistent.
- Participants are transient.
- **Work Items are transient.**

I think that is the logical conclusion of the architecture we've been building, and it distinguishes the platform from virtually every existing software engineering and project management system.

# 1. Purpose

A **Deliverable** represents a measurable engineering outcome produced by a Software Engineering Unit (SEU).

Deliverables are the primary execution objects of the platform.

The purpose of the Deliverable Model is to define how engineering outcomes are represented, evolved, governed and accepted throughout the lifecycle of an SEU.

Unlike traditional project management systems, where tasks are primary, the AI Software Organisation Platform treats Deliverables as the central objects of engineering execution.

---

# 4. Definition

A Deliverable is a persistent engineering artefact representing an expected outcome of an SEU.

A Deliverable shall:

- possess identity;
- possess lifecycle state;
- possess dependencies;
- possess acceptance criteria;
- possess an Acquisition Scope (§9);
- maintain engineering history.

Deliverables exist independently of Participants.

# 9. Deliverable Acquisition Scope

Every Deliverable shall declare an **Acquisition Scope**, determining how broadly the Knowledge, Evidence and Decisions it produces are intended to propagate once accepted.

## SEU

Propagates no further than the originating SEU.

The default Acquisition Scope where none is declared.

---

## Capability

Propagates to any SEU, within the same Tenant, that fulfils the same Capability.

Suitable for engineering understanding specific to how a Capability is exercised, rather than to any single engagement.

---

## Enterprise

Propagates to every SEU within the same Tenant, regardless of Capability.

Suitable for cross-cutting engineering understanding — a coding standard, an architectural principle, an operational practice — that applies broadly across the Tenant's engineering, not to one Capability alone.

---

## Platform

Propagates as a candidate for codification into a Platform Pack, available to every Tenant on the platform.

Platform Acquisition Scope is not self-propagating the way the other three are. It identifies Knowledge as a candidate for platform-wide reuse; it does not, by itself, cross Tenant boundaries. Promotion to Platform Pack content requires the same governed Pack-publication path any Platform Pack change requires (Chapter 39), respecting the Tenant isolation principles of Chapter 42 — no Tenant's engineering state is ever directly exposed to another Tenant as a side effect of this scope.

---

Capability, Enterprise and Platform Acquisition Scope together constitute **Engineering Capital** (see Chapter 16 §13 and `Terminology and Reconciliation.md` §6). SEU-scoped Knowledge does not, since it never outlives the SEU that produced it.

Acquisition Scope is declared on the Deliverable but may be promoted after the fact on the Knowledge it produced, where later experience shows understanding generalises further than originally assessed (Chapter 16 §12).

# 10. Deliverable Lifecycle

Every Deliverable shall transition through a defined lifecycle.

A default lifecycle is:

```
Defined

↓

Planned

↓

In Progress

↓

Under Review

↓

Approved

↓

Baselined

↓

Superseded

↓

Archived
```

The Engineering Behavior Model may define additional lifecycle states.

---

# 11. Deliverable State

State represents the engineering maturity of a Deliverable.

Typical state transitions include:

- Defined → Planned
- Planned → In Progress
- In Progress → Under Review
- Under Review → Approved
- Approved → Baselined

State transitions shall comply with the Engineering Behavior Model.

---

# 12. Deliverable Relationships

Deliverables may relate to one another through:

- dependency;
- derivation;
- refinement;
- validation;
- implementation;
- supersession;
- decomposition.

Relationship semantics shall be explicitly defined.

---

# 13. Deliverable Ownership

Deliverables are owned by the SEU.

Participants contribute to Deliverables but never own them.

Capability fulfilment may change.

Deliverable ownership does not.

---

# 14. Deliverable Acceptance

Every Deliverable shall define measurable acceptance criteria.

Acceptance may require:

- engineering review;
- evidence;
- obligations resolved;
- approval;
- automated validation.

Acceptance criteria are governed by the Engineering Behavior Model.

---

# 15. Deliverable Evolution

Deliverables evolve continuously throughout the SEU lifecycle.

Evolution shall preserve:

- engineering history;
- traceability;
- decision history;
- evidence;
- superseded versions.

Historical versions shall remain accessible.

---

# 16. Deliverable State Transitions

State transitions may be triggered by:

- completed execution;
- accepted decisions;
- approved evidence;
- resolved obligations;
- dependency satisfaction;
- governance approvals.

Transitions shall never occur without traceability.

---

# 18. Non-Functional Requirements

The Deliverable Model shall:

- support versioning;
- preserve complete engineering history;
- remain independent of Participants;
- support concurrent evolution;
- maintain deterministic state transitions.

---

# 19. Acceptance Criteria

The implementation shall satisfy the following criteria.

✓ Deliverables possess unique identities.

✓ Deliverables evolve through governed state transitions.

✓ Deliverables maintain engineering history.

✓ Deliverables participate in dependency evaluation.

✓ Deliverables preserve traceability.

✓ Deliverables remain independent of Participant replacement.

✓ Every Deliverable declares an Acquisition Scope, and Knowledge it produces propagates accordingly.
