# Editorial Log — Book 1 Refinement

This log tracks the refinement pass over Book 1. It is not part of the manuscript. It exists so that:

- you can see *why* something changed without diffing prose line-by-line (the sentence-level rewrites are often too pervasive for a diff to be readable),
- issues that span multiple chapters are captured once, in one place, instead of being silently patched wherever they're noticed, and
- the cumulative "manifesto" (per purpose.md's instruction that chapter summaries build on one another) has a single source of truth.

Originals are untouched in `01_Book 1/`. Refined chapters are written into `01_Book 1 (Refined)/`, mirroring the same folder structure — **with one deliberate exception from 2026-08-01 onward**: Chapters 5-7 have moved from `01_Part 1/` to `02_Part 2/` in the Refined folder as part of firming up the book's overall architecture (see below), so the Refined folder's Part boundaries no longer match the original manuscript's. Chapter numbers themselves are unchanged throughout — only which Part folder a chapter sits in has moved.

## Style rules being applied (from purpose.md)

1. **Chapter summaries → declarative/manifesto style.** Each chapter's closing summary section ends with a short numbered list of aphoristic statements (as modelled by Chapter 31's manifesto), not prose paragraphs.
2. **Body prose → academic narrative.** Springer/MIT Press/Cambridge monograph register. Ideas should connect through content, not announced transitions.
3. **Reduce signposting phrases** — explicitly called out in purpose.md: "This distinction is important," "This observation is significant," "Consequently...," "This principle...," "This perspective..." These appear as near-universal sentence-openers throughout the current draft (I count dozens per chapter in Ch1–7 alone). Removing all of them is a substantial rewrite, not a light edit — flagging this so the scale of change per chapter is expected, not a surprise.
4. **[Sudha: ...] remarks** are being folded into the running prose (not left as bracketed asides). Where a remark contains a fully drafted replacement passage already sitting in the text (see below), the replacement is treated as canonical and the superseded draft + the bracketed commentary explaining it are retired.
5. **In-manuscript review tags.** Where something is kept in the text as-is but its status is genuinely unresolved (not a style call I can just make), it gets a visible italicised marker in the chapter itself — `*[Review — <topic>: <why it's open> See Editorial Log.]*` — distinct from your own `[Sudha: ...]` notes. Nothing gets silently marked "Resolved" in this log unless it actually is; if you correct that judgement, the log gets fixed too, not just the chapter.
6. **Autonomous mode (from 2026-07-30).** Per Sudha's instruction, proceeding through the rest of Book 1 without pausing for confirmation between chapters. Anything genuinely uncertain — a judgement call, a cross-chapter theory question, a possible author intent I can't verify from the text — gets flagged here (and tagged `[Review]` in-chapter per rule 5) rather than raised as a question. Sudha is reviewing in parallel, asynchronously, not gating each chapter.
7. **This is a multi-pass process, not a linear one.** Confirmed by you directly: your thinking on Ch1 was itself refined while drafting Ch5 (e.g. Objective/Capability didn't exist as concepts yet when Ch1 was written). So a chapter being "refined" here means *drafted to the best of what's been read so far* — not finished. Once a later chapter clarifies or supersedes something an earlier chapter assumed, the earlier chapter needs a revisit. Chapter status below now distinguishes "Drafted" from "Confirmed" for this reason. Remarks marked `[Sudha: ...]` in any given chapter may likewise imply changes elsewhere in the book, not just locally — flagging this rather than assuming a remark's effect stops at its host chapter.

## Known structural issue: layered drafts in Ch 4–7

Chapters 4 through 7 (and possibly beyond, not yet checked) contain a recurring pattern: original prose, followed by a `[Sudha: ...]` note critiquing it and proposing a rewrite, **followed immediately by the actual rewritten section already drafted in place** — sometimes with a second round of the same pattern layered on top of that (e.g. Chapter 5 §5.4–5.9 effectively contains two or three successive drafts of the same section stacked in sequence; Chapter 6 §6.2, 6.6, 6.8 similarly). 

Working assumption: **the last (most downstream) version in the file is canonical** — it represents your most recent thinking — and everything upstream of it (the earlier draft + the commentary announcing the rewrite) is retired rather than merged. I'll flag here if a case looks ambiguous instead of guessing.

## Running Manifesto (cumulative, built chapter by chapter)

**After Chapter 1:**
1. Software is engineered by organisations. Programming is one capability performed within them.
2. Large software systems are built by software engineering organisations, not by programmers working in isolation.
3. The Software Engineering Unit — not the autonomous agent, not the enterprise as a whole — is the fundamental unit of software engineering.
4. Organisational capability, not individual expertise, sustains software systems across decades of change.
5. Artificial intelligence becomes a participant within the organisation; it does not become the organisation.

**After Chapter 2:**
6. Automating engineering activities is not the same as modelling the organisation that performs them.
7. Conversation between AI systems produces collaboration, not organisation.
8. Roles, authority, accountability, institutional memory, governance and traceability are properties of organisations; no accumulation of agent conversation creates them.
9. Source code records what was decided; organisational knowledge records why — and knowledge outlasts code.
10. An autonomous engineering organisation is measured by what it learns from each project, not by how quickly it completes the next one.

**After Chapter 3:**
11. The AI Software Engineering Unit is defined by its organisational structures, not by the intelligence of its participants.
12. A role is a sustained responsibility; a participant is whoever currently discharges it — the two are never the same thing.
13. Autonomy without governance is organisational risk; autonomy under governance is organisational capability.
14. The organisation inherits its principles from mature human engineering practice, not its practices — many of which exist only to compensate for human limitation.
15. Engineering continuity survives every participant, every technology and every project the organisation completes; it does not survive being defined as any one of them.

**After Chapter 4:**
16. An organisation's identity lives in its Operating Model, not in whoever currently staffs it.
17. Human organisational practice compensates for human limitation; once the participants are artificial, the compensations are optional, not the organisation itself.
18. Expertise that can be replicated stops being the scarce resource — governance of that expertise becomes scarce instead.
19. What an AI-native organisation forgets is a policy choice, not a limitation.
20. A stable Operating Model, not a fixed set of participants, is what lets an organisation absorb every future generation of AI without being redesigned by it.

**After Chapter 5:**
21. An organisation is not a hierarchy of roles; it is a purpose-driven network in which every construct traces back to an objective.
22. A capability is what the organisation can do; a competency is what a participant can do — confusing the two is how organisations end up dependent on individuals they cannot replace.
23. An artefact is not knowledge, and a claim is not evidence — treating either as settled fact is how organisations mistake documentation for understanding.
24. A decision that cannot be traced to the evidence that justified it is not a decision an organisation can trust with its own history.
25. The organisation does not just pursue its objectives — it revises its understanding of them through everything it learns while trying to achieve them.

**After Chapter 6:**
26. A capability is what an organisation can do independently of who currently does it; lose sight of that distinction and the organisation becomes hostage to whoever it happens to employ.
27. Two organisations can run identical technology and process and still differ completely in capability — the difference is what makes one worth studying and the other not.
28. Roles exist to be accountable for capabilities; capability owners exist to make capabilities better — neither is a manager, and confusing capability ownership with management is how stewardship quietly disappears.
29. A capability that cannot be measured except by counting the work performed around it has not actually been measured.
30. An organisation does not get smarter by hiring smarter participants — it gets smarter by turning what those participants learn into capability that outlives them.

**After Chapter 7 (Part 1 complete):**
31. A capability describes what an organisation can do; a service describes what it actually delivers to someone else — an organisation only becomes real to the rest of itself through the second, never the first alone.
32. An activity is what happens inside a capability; a service is what the rest of the organisation is allowed to depend on — collapse that boundary and every internal change becomes everyone's problem.
33. Two capabilities that never exchange a service are not actually part of the same organisation, whatever the org chart says.
34. A service that cannot be evaluated except by asking the people who provided it has no contract worth the name.
35. An organisation's org chart shows who has authority; its service network shows how it stays alive — mistake the first for the second and you can reorganise forever without changing anything that matters.
