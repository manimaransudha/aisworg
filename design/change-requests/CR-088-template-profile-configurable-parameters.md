# CR-088 — Template/Profile configurable parameters (Book 3 Ch.6 §13)

**Raised:** 2026-09-05 · **Origin:** owner, reviewing Chapter 6 (Template) §18 Acceptance Criteria before moving to Profile — "There is a gap that is required for profiles to work correctly." · **Status:** 🟡 Proposed (design settled so far for Capability, Service, Checklist, and Policy; Quality Gates/Review Gates/Authority Rules not yet walked; not built).

## The gap (confirmed live, 2026-09-05)

Chapter 6 §13: "Templates may expose configurable parameters. Examples: Development methodology, Technology stack, Target environment, Domain selection, Compliance requirements, Organisation Packs. These parameters are supplied during commissioning." Checked against the actual build — not partially built, entirely unbuilt:

- **Template's schema has nothing for it.** `schema_definitions` (Template v1) properties are just `code, name, purpose, templateVersion, deliverableCatalogue, dependencyGraph`, plus the six category-scoped mandatory-Pack fields. No declared parameter slots anywhere.
- **Profile's `configParameters` is untyped, unvalidated, free-form JSON.** `core/profiles.ts`/`profilesDB.ts` just `JSON.stringify` whatever's submitted straight into the `config_parameters` column — no schema check, no connection back to whatever Template `baseTemplateCode` points at.
- **Nothing downstream ever reads it.** Grepped `compositionEngine`/`commissioning.ts`/the whole `domain/` layer — the only other hit is `formGenerator.ts` bucketing it into the generic metadata field group so it renders as a plain JSON textarea in the authoring form. Accepted, stored, never consumed again.
- **Packs have no parameter concept either**, which is *why* Template can't expose anything: a Pack's own shape (`code, name, category, packVersion, installationClassification, description, owner, publisher, dependencies`, plus its `contributions`) has no configurable slot a consumer fills in — it's a fixed, versioned, published unit. Template, as "just a group of Packs and Deliverables," has nothing upstream of it to reach into and expose downstream (owner: "The template is just a group of packs and deliverables. It does not know what the parameters of the pack are for it to expose it downstream").
- §13's own five examples split once checked this way: **Technology stack, Domain selection, Compliance requirements, Organisation Packs** already exist as *which Pack, from a category, gets selected* — the category-scoped `technologyPackCodes`/`domainPackCodes`/`compliancePackCodes`/`organisationPackCodes` fields on both Template (mandatory) and Profile (optional). **Development methodology** doesn't map onto anything built.

## Design (owner, settled so far)

> "This is how we are going to fix it. For each of the component of a pack, we are going to define if that component has a configurable parameter."

Walking Pack's component types one at a time:

- **Capability — no configuration.** "Capability is just an ontology term - so no configuration there." A Capability is a pure Ontology `capability-name` shell (code + label), no shape of its own to parameterize.
- **Service — the Service Level *is* the configurable parameter.** "Service levels are the configurable parameters. Service definition can say 90% (that is the enterprise goal). A template can say 80% (just being realistic). The profile should be able to say 60% (tuned further for specific nature of work)." Refined after cross-checking against the Pack-level SLA override that already exists (`contributionServices[].serviceLevel[]`, target-only override, `label`/`units`/`target_level` inherited read-only from the Service Definition, the Definition's own row never overwritten) into a **four-layer cascade**, corrected once mid-discussion (owner: "Let me correct myself. I might have mixed things up"):
  1. **Service Definition** — the canonical/enterprise value (e.g. 90%). Definition.
  2. **Pack** — may override it with its own real value (already built, unchanged, no conflict with this design after the correction).
  3. **Template** — does not supply a number. Its job is to *flag* which of its selected Packs' service-level metrics are open for further tuning — a declaration ("this one is a configurable parameter"), not a value.
  4. **Profile** — supplies the actual override *value* for whichever metrics Template exposed as configurable. This is where the "60%" in the example actually lands, not at Template.
- **All four layers stay Definition, not Execution** — no matter how many layers of override stack up, none of it is an execution-dimension fact by itself (owner: "The override is all still definition unless something is commissioned against the profile"). Resolution into one concrete, effective value only happens once a specific SEU is actually commissioned (Template + Profile + resolved Packs → EBM) — the same moment `compositionEngine.compose` already resolves Authority Rules/Policies/Quality Gates into an EBM's own execution-facing shape today. Checked directly: `compositionEngine.ts` currently has zero handling of `serviceLevel` at all — this resolution doesn't exist anywhere yet, not even partially.

This reframes the real gap as two concrete pieces, not one: **Template needs an authoring surface** to mark specific service-level metrics (from whatever its selected Packs actually carry) as configurable — nothing like that exists today. **Profile's `configParameters` needs to become a structured mechanism** that supplies values *against exactly those flagged parameters* — replacing the current free-form, unvalidated, never-read JSON blob with something that has a real source and a real consumer.

- **Checklist — a filterable tag per item, not a value override.** A different shape from Service Level: the configurable thing isn't a single number to tune, it's *which items even apply*. A Checklist item today is exactly `statement` (required, markdown) + `group` (optional, organisational label — confirmed against the live schema, `schema_definitions` Pack v1, `contributionChecklists.items.items.properties`) — nothing else. Owner: "In addition to group and a statement of the item, let us add an object called `{configurable: {type: required/mandatory/conditional, property2: config1/config2..}}`" — a third field, `configurable`, an object whose keys are whatever named dimensions a Pack author chooses per item (not a fixed set — one Pack might tag `type`, another might tag something unrelated).
  - **Template exposes every key under `configurable` mechanically** — unlike Service Level, there's no separate Template-level "pick which ones matter" step; whatever properties exist on any item from its selected Packs' Checklists all surface as configurable parameters automatically. Owner's own framing: "Imagine the configuration parameters to be excel columns that are filterable."
  - **Profile picks a filter value per exposed property** (e.g. `type = required`), and at commissioning the EBM includes only the checklist items whose `configurable.type` matches — "So when a profile says required, then the EBM picks all checklist items filtered for 'required'."
  - **Default is "all," on both sides** (owner: "When there is no configurable object or no configurable parameter, it is 'all'"): an item with no `configurable` object at all is always included, unconditionally, regardless of any filter a Profile applies. A property an item *does* carry but a Profile never sets a filter value for behaves the same way for that one dimension — unset means pass-through, not exclude. Filtering only narrows the set when a Profile actively selects a value *and* the item actually carries that same property to compare against; absence on either side always defaults to inclusion — the same behaviour an unset Excel column filter has (shows every row, hides none).

- **Policy — two shapes at once, walked as part of [CR-089](../../change-requests/CR-089-canonical-policy-definitions.md)'s own canonical-catalog build (2026-09-05); the schema markers are real, the cascade/filter behind them is not.** Ch.24's new `policy_definitions` catalog carries four fields relevant here, splitting into the same two shapes already settled above — not a third, new one:
  - **`constraintType` (Policy/Standard, Ch.24 §4) is Service-Level-shaped** — a single value to tune, not a set to filter. Same reasoning as Service Level: the canonical Definition sets the enterprise-default (e.g. "Policy," mandatory), Template would flag it as open for tuning, Profile would supply the override (e.g. relaxing it to "Standard," or tightening a "Standard" to "Policy," in a stricter Profile). **One layer shorter than Service's own four-layer cascade, though**: Service's cascade has a real Pack-level override step already built (`contributionServices[].serviceLevel[]`); Policy's own Pack-adoption mechanism (`contributionPolicies[]`, CR-089) is a pure `{code}` reference with no per-adoption override field at all — so today the chain is Definition → *(no Pack step)* → Template (unbuilt) → Profile (unbuilt). Not a deliberate exclusion of the Pack layer, just not asked for when `contributionPolicies[]` was built as a plain checkbox picker.
  - **`applicabilityDeliverableNames` / `applicabilityEnvironments` / `applicabilityDeliverableLifecycle` (Ch.24 §9) are Checklist-shaped** — each a *list*, narrowed by which values apply rather than tuned to one number, and each already defaults empty-means-"all" (Ch.24's own established convention, the same default rule Checklist's own `configurable` settled on independently).
  - **Built so far**: all four fields carry `x-configurable: true` on the real schema (`167_policy_definitions.sql`) — the same "discoverable by inspecting the schema, not a comment" marker this CR's own Checklist design already achieves via its `configurable` object, satisfying "how will downstream systems know this is configurable" structurally.
  - **Not built, same open items as Service Level and Checklist below**: no Template-side "expose as configurable" flag for any of the four, no Profile-side override/filter mechanism, and `compositionEngine` resolves none of it. Chapter 24 §19.12 tracks this same gap from the Policy side; recorded here too so it isn't lost from the Template/Profile side of the design.

## Explicitly not decided here

- **The remaining Pack component types** — Quality Gates, Review Gates, Authority Rules — haven't been walked yet the way Capability, Service, Checklist, and Policy were. Whether any of them has something analogous to a "configurable parameter" is open.
- The concrete schema/mechanism for Template's own "expose as configurable" flag for Service Level (per service-level metric? per Service? does it live on the Pack-selection fields, a new field, something else?) — Checklist doesn't need this same flag, since exposure there is automatic off whatever `configurable` keys a Pack's own items already carry.
- The concrete shape Profile's parameter-value mechanism should take for both Service Level (a value per exposed metric) and Checklist (a filter value per exposed property) — replacing today's free-form `configParameters` blob — and whether that blob is retired outright or migrated, or whether one structure ends up covering both shapes.
- How `compositionEngine.compose` needs to change to actually resolve the Service Level cascade into one effective value, and to filter Checklist items by Profile's selected values, per SEU at commissioning time.
- Whether `configurable`'s per-property values are free strings (as the `required/mandatory/conditional` example suggests) or themselves need to be a controlled/enumerated vocabulary the way other authored fields in this system generally are.
- Whether Development Methodology (the one §13 example that maps onto nothing built) is a missing sixth category-scoped Pack-selection slot, or something this new configurable-parameter mechanism should carry instead.

Not building yet — recorded so the settled parts of the design (Capability/Service/Checklist/Policy) and the open parts (everything else) aren't lost before the rest of Pack's component types get walked and Profile itself gets looked at.
