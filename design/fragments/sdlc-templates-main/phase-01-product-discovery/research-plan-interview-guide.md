## Template Overview

The interview guide provides structured questions to gather consistent, actionable insights from research participants. Use this to validate personas, jobs-to-be-done, and product assumptions.*

# Research Plan

## Research Objectives

### Primary Research Questions
1. **Problem Validation**: Do our target users experience the knowledge management problems we've identified?
2. **Solution Fit**: How well does our proposed solution address their current pain points?
3. **Persona Validation**: Are our user personas accurate representations of our target market?
4. **Feature Prioritization**: Which features are most critical for user adoption and success?
5. **Competitive Landscape**: How do users currently solve these problems, and what are the limitations?

### Secondary Research Questions
1. What are the organizational barriers to adopting new knowledge management tools?
2. How do different roles within organizations approach knowledge sharing differently?
3. What security and compliance requirements are non-negotiable for enterprise users?
4. How do teams currently measure the success of knowledge management initiatives?

## Research Methodology

### Approach: Mixed Methods
- **Qualitative Research**: In-depth interviews for deep insights and context
- **Quantitative Research**: Survey for broader validation and statistical significance
- **Observational Research**: Workflow analysis to understand current behaviors

### Research Activities

#### Phase 1: Exploratory Interviews (Weeks 1-2)
- **Objective**: Understand current pain points and workflows
- **Method**: 60-minute semi-structured interviews
- **Sample Size**: 15 participants across 3 personas
- **Format**: Video calls with screen sharing for workflow demonstration

#### Phase 2: Solution Validation (Weeks 3-4)
- **Objective**: Test product concepts and feature priorities
- **Method**: 45-minute concept testing interviews
- **Sample Size**: 12 participants (subset from Phase 1)
- **Format**: Interactive prototype testing with think-aloud protocol

#### Phase 3: Quantitative Validation (Week 5)
- **Objective**: Validate findings at scale
- **Method**: Online survey
- **Sample Size**: 200+ enterprise knowledge workers
- **Format**: 10-minute survey with screening questions

## Participant Recruitment

### Target Participants

#### Primary Persona: Knowledge Workers (60% of sample)
- **Role**: Individual contributors in marketing, sales, consulting, or project management
- **Company Size**: 100-5,000 employees
- **Experience**: 3+ years in current role
- **Screening**: Currently uses multiple tools for note-taking and knowledge sharing

#### Secondary Persona: Team Leads (30% of sample)
- **Role**: Managers or team leads with 5+ direct reports
- **Company Size**: 500-10,000 employees
- **Experience**: 2+ years in management role
- **Screening**: Responsible for team knowledge management and collaboration

#### Tertiary Persona: IT Administrators (10% of sample)
- **Role**: IT administrators or security professionals
- **Company Size**: 1,000+ employees
- **Experience**: 3+ years in enterprise IT
- **Screening**: Involved in enterprise software evaluation and implementation

### Recruitment Channels
1. **Professional Networks**: LinkedIn outreach to target personas
2. **Industry Communities**: Slack groups, forums, and professional associations
3. **Customer Referrals**: Existing contacts and warm introductions
4. **Research Panels**: UserInterviews.com, Respondent.io for targeted recruitment
5. **Conference Attendees**: Participants from relevant industry events

### Incentives
- **Individual Contributors**: $75 Amazon gift card per interview
- **Managers/Team Leads**: $100 Amazon gift card per interview
- **IT Administrators**: $125 Amazon gift card per interview
- **Survey Participants**: Entry into $500 gift card raffle

## Research Timeline

### Week 1: Preparation & Recruitment
- Finalize interview guide and research materials
- Begin participant recruitment across all channels
- Set up research tools (Calendly, Zoom, recording software)
- Create participant database and tracking system

### Week 2: Exploratory Interviews
- Conduct 8-10 exploratory interviews
- Daily debrief sessions to capture initial insights
- Adjust interview guide based on early learnings
- Continue recruitment for remaining participants

### Week 3: Continued Interviews & Analysis
- Complete remaining exploratory interviews
- Begin preliminary analysis and insight synthesis
- Prepare concept testing materials for Phase 2
- Recruit Phase 2 participants from Phase 1 pool

### Week 4: Solution Validation
- Conduct concept testing interviews
- Test key user flows and feature priorities
- Gather feedback on pricing and positioning
- Begin survey design based on interview insights

### Week 5: Quantitative Validation
- Launch and promote survey to broader audience
- Monitor response rates and adjust outreach as needed
- Conduct final follow-up interviews if needed
- Begin comprehensive analysis of all research data

### Week 6: Analysis & Reporting
- Complete data analysis and insight synthesis
- Create research findings report with recommendations
- Present findings to product and leadership teams
- Update personas and product requirements based on insights

# Interview Guide

## Pre-Interview Setup (5 minutes)

### Logistics
- [ ] Test recording equipment and backup systems
- [ ] Confirm participant consent for recording
- [ ] Have incentive information ready
- [ ] Prepare note-taking template

### Introduction Script
*"Hi [Name], thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. I'm [Your Name] from [Company], and I'm conducting research to better understand how teams manage and share knowledge in their organizations. This conversation will take about 60 minutes, and I'd like to record it so I can focus on our conversation rather than taking notes. Is that okay with you?"*

*"Just to set expectations, I'm not trying to sell you anything today. I'm genuinely interested in learning about your experiences and challenges. There are no right or wrong answers, and your honest feedback is incredibly valuable to us."*

## Section 1: Background & Context (10 minutes)

### Role & Responsibilities
1. "Can you start by telling me about your role and what a typical day looks like for you?"
2. "How much of your work involves creating, sharing, or finding information?"
3. "Who do you collaborate with most frequently, and how large is your immediate team?"

### Current Tools & Workflows
4. "What tools do you currently use for taking notes, creating documents, or sharing information?"
5. "Walk me through how you typically capture information during a meeting or project."
6. "How do you organize and store information so you can find it later?"

### Pain Points Discovery
7. "Tell me about a recent time when you struggled to find information you knew existed somewhere."
8. "What happens when someone on your team leaves? How do you handle knowledge transfer?"
9. "Describe a situation where poor information sharing caused problems for your team."

## Section 2: Current State Analysis (15 minutes)

### Knowledge Capture Behaviors
10. "Show me how you typically take notes during a meeting." *[Ask for screen share if virtual]*
11. "What types of information do you find most important to capture and preserve?"
12. "How do you decide what information is worth documenting versus what you can remember?"

### Sharing & Collaboration Patterns
13. "Tell me about the last time you needed to share important information with your team."
14. "How do you currently collaborate on documents or projects that require input from multiple people?"
15. "What makes you more or less likely to share knowledge with your colleagues?"

### Search & Discovery Challenges
16. "When you need to find specific information, what's your typical process?"
17. "How often do you end up recreating work that you think might already exist somewhere?"
18. "What are the biggest obstacles to finding the information you need?"

### Organizational Context
19. "How does your organization currently approach knowledge management?"
20. "What are the biggest knowledge management challenges your team faces?"
21. "Are there any security or compliance requirements that affect how you handle information?"

## Section 3: Ideal State Exploration (15 minutes)

### Vision & Aspirations
22. "If you could wave a magic wand and solve your biggest knowledge management challenge, what would that look like?"
23. "Describe your ideal workflow for capturing, organizing, and sharing information."
24. "What would need to be true for your team to be 50% more effective at knowledge sharing?"

### Feature Prioritization
25. "If you had a tool that could do three things perfectly, what would those three things be?"
26. "What features would be absolutely essential versus nice-to-have?"
27. "How important is real-time collaboration versus individual productivity?"

### Success Metrics
28. "How would you measure whether a knowledge management solution was successful?"
29. "What would convince your manager that investing in better knowledge tools was worthwhile?"
30. "How long would you expect it to take to see benefits from a new knowledge management system?"

## Section 4: Solution Validation (10 minutes)

*Note: This section is used in Phase 2 interviews with prototype or concept materials*

### Concept Reaction
31. "I'm going to show you some concepts for a knowledge management platform. What's your initial reaction?"
32. "What aspects of this solution are most appealing to you?"
33. "What concerns or questions does this raise for you?"

### Feature Feedback
34. "Which of these features would you use most frequently?"
35. "Are there any features that seem unnecessary or confusing?"
36. "What's missing that would be important for your workflow?"

### Adoption Considerations
37. "What would it take for you to switch from your current tools to something like this?"
38. "What barriers do you see to your team adopting a new knowledge management platform?"
39. "Who else would need to be involved in evaluating a solution like this?"

## Section 5: Wrap-up & Next Steps (5 minutes)

### Final Thoughts
40. "Is there anything important about knowledge management that we haven't discussed?"
41. "What advice would you give to someone building a tool for teams like yours?"
42. "Are there other people on your team who might have different perspectives on these challenges?"

### Follow-up
43. "Would you be interested in seeing how this research influences our product development?"
44. "Can I follow up with you in a few months to get your feedback on what we build?"
45. "Do you have any questions for me about this research or our company?"

### Closing
*"Thank you so much for your time and insights today. This has been incredibly valuable, and I really appreciate your honesty and thoughtfulness. I'll send you the gift card information via email, and please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions or additional thoughts."*

## Research Analysis Framework

### Data Collection
- **Interview Recordings**: Transcribed and coded for themes
- **Observation Notes**: Behavioral insights and workflow documentation
- **Survey Responses**: Quantitative validation of qualitative findings
- **Artifact Collection**: Screenshots, documents, and workflow examples

### Analysis Methods

#### Thematic Analysis
1. **Open Coding**: Identify initial themes and patterns
2. **Axial Coding**: Group related themes and identify relationships
3. **Selective Coding**: Develop core insights and recommendations

#### Persona Validation
- Compare research findings to existing persona assumptions
- Identify gaps or inaccuracies in persona definitions
- Update personas based on validated insights

#### Jobs-to-be-Done Validation
- Map user statements to existing JTBD framework
- Identify new jobs or refine existing job definitions
- Prioritize jobs based on frequency and importance

### Insight Synthesis
- **Pain Point Prioritization**: Rank problems by frequency and impact
- **Feature Validation**: Confirm or challenge product assumptions
- **Opportunity Identification**: Discover unmet needs and market gaps
- **Risk Assessment**: Identify potential adoption barriers and concerns
