# Jobs-to-be-Done Statements
**Phase**: 01 - Product Discovery (aka: Discovery Sprint, Exploration Sprint, Problem/Solution Fit, Inception, Validation Sprint)
**Deliverable Type**: Jobs-to-be-Done Analysis
**Template Purpose**: Define the functional, emotional, and social jobs customers hire your product to do
**Last Updated**: November 2025

## Template Overview

*Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) statements capture what customers are trying to accomplish, the context in which they're trying to accomplish it, and the outcome they seek. Use the format: "When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]." Focus on the job, not the solution.*

---

## Primary Jobs-to-be-Done

### Job 1: Capture and Preserve Knowledge
**When I** have insights, decisions, or learnings from meetings, projects, or conversations,
**I want to** quickly capture and organize this information in a searchable, structured way,
**So I can** preserve valuable knowledge and easily retrieve it when needed for future decisions or projects.

**Job Context**:
- During or immediately after meetings
- While working on projects or solving problems
- When discovering best practices or lessons learned
- During knowledge transfer sessions

**Success Criteria**:
- Information is captured within 2 minutes of the insight
- Content is automatically organized and tagged
- Knowledge is searchable and discoverable by relevant team members
- Information remains accessible even when original creator leaves

**Current Alternatives**:
- Email to self with meeting notes
- Google Docs with scattered information
- Handwritten notes that get lost
- Slack messages that disappear in history

---

### Job 2: Share Knowledge Across Teams
**When I** have information, insights, or resources that could benefit my colleagues,
**I want to** easily share this knowledge with the right people at the right time,
**So I can** improve team productivity and prevent others from duplicating work or making the same mistakes.

**Job Context**:
- Onboarding new team members
- Starting similar projects or campaigns
- Solving problems that others have encountered
- Sharing best practices across departments

**Success Criteria**:
- Knowledge sharing takes less than 1 minute
- Recipients can easily find and understand shared information
- Sharing permissions are granular and secure
- Shared knowledge is discoverable by future team members

**Current Alternatives**:
- Email with attachments and explanations
- Slack messages with links to various documents
- Verbal knowledge transfer in meetings
- Creating duplicate documents for different audiences

---

### Job 3: Collaborate on Documentation
**When I** need to work with others to create, review, or improve documentation,
**I want to** collaborate in real-time with clear visibility into changes and contributions,
**So I can** produce higher-quality documentation faster while maintaining clear ownership and accountability.

**Job Context**:
- Creating project documentation with team members
- Reviewing and improving existing processes
- Collaborative planning and brainstorming sessions
- Cross-functional project coordination

**Success Criteria**:
- Multiple people can edit simultaneously without conflicts
- All changes are tracked with clear attribution
- Comments and feedback are contextual and actionable
- Final version reflects all stakeholder input

**Current Alternatives**:
- Google Docs with version control issues
- Email chains with document attachments
- Sequential editing with long feedback cycles
- Meeting-based collaboration with manual note-taking

---

### Job 4: Find Relevant Information Quickly
**When I** need specific information, examples, or context to complete my work,
**I want to** search across all organizational knowledge and quickly find exactly what I need,
**So I can** make informed decisions and complete tasks without delays or duplicated effort.

**Job Context**:
- Starting new projects and needing historical context
- Solving problems that may have been addressed before
- Looking for templates, examples, or best practices
- Researching decisions or strategies from past initiatives

**Success Criteria**:
- Relevant information found within 30 seconds
- Search results are ranked by relevance and recency
- Context and source of information is clear
- Related information is suggested automatically

**Current Alternatives**:
- Asking colleagues via Slack or email
- Searching through multiple tools and platforms
- Recreating work that already exists somewhere
- Making decisions without full context

## Secondary Jobs-to-be-Done

### Job 5: Maintain Organizational Memory
**When I** am concerned about losing important knowledge when team members leave or change roles,
**I want to** ensure critical information is documented and accessible to the organization,
**So I can** maintain continuity and prevent knowledge loss that impacts future performance.

**Job Context**:
- Employee transitions and departures
- Role changes and team restructuring
- Project handoffs and transfers
- Long-term strategic planning

**Success Criteria**:
- Critical knowledge is identified and documented
- Information is accessible to appropriate successors
- Knowledge transfer is systematic, not ad-hoc
- Organizational learning is preserved over time

---

### Job 6: Standardize Team Processes
**When I** want to ensure consistency in how my team approaches common tasks or projects,
**I want to** create and maintain standardized templates and processes,
**So I can** improve quality, reduce errors, and accelerate team performance.

**Job Context**:
- Creating repeatable workflows
- Onboarding new team members
- Scaling successful approaches
- Ensuring compliance and quality standards

**Success Criteria**:
- Templates are easily accessible and up-to-date
- Team members consistently use standardized approaches
- Processes can be improved based on usage and feedback
- New team members can quickly adopt established practices

---

### Job 7: Demonstrate Impact and ROI
**When I** need to show the value of my team's work or justify resource allocation,
**I want to** easily compile evidence of our contributions, decisions, and outcomes,
**So I can** communicate our impact to stakeholders and secure continued support.

**Job Context**:
- Performance reviews and team assessments
- Budget planning and resource requests
- Project retrospectives and lessons learned
- Stakeholder reporting and communication

**Success Criteria**:
- Work history and contributions are easily documented
- Impact metrics and outcomes are tracked over time
- Success stories and case studies can be quickly compiled
- Evidence supports strategic decision-making

---

## Emotional Jobs

### Job 8: Feel Confident in Decision-Making
**When I** need to make important decisions that affect my team or projects,
**I want to** have access to relevant historical context, data, and expert insights,
**So I can** feel confident that I'm making informed decisions based on organizational knowledge.

**Emotional Drivers**:
- Reduce anxiety about making wrong decisions
- Increase confidence in recommendations to leadership
- Feel supported by organizational knowledge
- Avoid embarrassment from missing important context

---

### Job 9: Feel Valued for Knowledge Contributions
**When I** share my expertise and insights with the organization,
**I want to** see that my contributions are recognized, used, and appreciated,
**So I can** feel motivated to continue sharing knowledge and contributing to team success.

**Emotional Drivers**:
- Recognition for expertise and contributions
- Sense of impact on team and organizational success
- Professional growth through knowledge leadership
- Pride in building organizational capabilities

---

## Social Jobs

### Job 10: Build Professional Reputation
**When I** consistently contribute valuable knowledge and insights to my organization,
**I want to** be recognized as a subject matter expert and trusted advisor,
**So I can** advance my career and increase my influence within the organization.

**Social Drivers**:
- Recognition as a knowledge leader
- Increased visibility with senior leadership
- Opportunities for career advancement
- Influence on strategic decisions and direction

## Job Prioritization Matrix

### High Frequency + High Impact
1. **Capture and Preserve Knowledge** - Daily activity with long-term value
2. **Find Relevant Information Quickly** - Multiple times daily, critical for productivity
3. **Share Knowledge Across Teams** - Regular activity that multiplies impact

### High Impact + Lower Frequency
4. **Collaborate on Documentation** - Project-based but critical for quality
5. **Maintain Organizational Memory** - Ongoing concern with strategic importance

### Supporting Jobs
6. **Standardize Team Processes** - Enables other jobs to be done more effectively
7. **Demonstrate Impact and ROI** - Important for organizational support
8. **Feel Confident in Decision-Making** - Emotional enabler for all other jobs

## Job Validation Questions

### For Customer Interviews
1. "Tell me about the last time you needed to find information to complete a task. What was that experience like?"
2. "How do you currently capture and organize important insights from your work?"
3. "Describe a situation where you wished you had better access to your team's collective knowledge."
4. "What happens when someone on your team leaves? How do you handle knowledge transfer?"
5. "How do you currently collaborate with others on documentation or planning?"

### For Product Validation
1. Does our solution help customers complete these jobs faster?
2. Does our solution reduce the effort required for these jobs?
3. Does our solution improve the quality of outcomes for these jobs?
4. Are there jobs we're not addressing that are equally important?
5. How do customers currently measure success for these jobs?

## Competitive Job Analysis

### How Competitors Address These Jobs

**Google Workspace**:
- Strong on collaboration (Job 3)
- Weak on knowledge organization and discovery (Jobs 1, 4)
- Limited organizational memory features (Job 5)

**Notion**:
- Good for individual knowledge capture (Job 1)
- Strong template and standardization features (Job 6)
- Weak on enterprise sharing and security (Job 2)

**Confluence**:
- Strong on organizational memory (Job 5)
- Good search and discovery features (Job 4)
- Complex interface reduces adoption (Jobs 1, 2)

**Microsoft 365**:
- Integrated collaboration tools (Job 3)
- Good search across applications (Job 4)
- Fragmented experience across multiple tools (Jobs 1, 2)

## Product Implications

### Feature Priorities Based on Jobs
1. **Quick Capture Interface** - Enable Job 1 with minimal friction
2. **Intelligent Search** - Solve Job 4 with AI-powered discovery
3. **Seamless Sharing** - Address Job 2 with granular permissions
4. **Real-time Collaboration** - Support Job 3 with conflict-free editing
5. **Knowledge Preservation** - Enable Job 5 with automated organization

### Success Metrics Aligned to Jobs
- **Time to Capture**: How quickly users can save information (Job 1)
- **Search Success Rate**: Percentage of searches that find relevant results (Job 4)
- **Sharing Frequency**: How often users share knowledge with others (Job 2)
- **Collaboration Engagement**: Multi-author document creation rate (Job 3)
- **Knowledge Retention**: Information accessibility after creator departure (Job 5)
